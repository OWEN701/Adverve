3 Places Mortgage Leads Go Cold

(and what the data actually says about fixing each one)

01

The first 5 minutes

Leads contacted within 5 minutes convert 21x more often than leads contacted after 30 minutes.

78% of borrowers go with whoever responds first — not whoever offers the best rate. Speed decides who gets the conversation before the pitch even starts. For most brokerages this size, the gap isn't skill or rate competitiveness — it's simply who picks up the phone first.

Source: Harvard Business Review, “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads” (2011); Velocify mortgage lead response research.

02

After attempt 2 or 3

Most loan officers stop following up after 2–3 touches. Real conversion takes 6–12 touches over 30 days.

Leads marked “dead” at attempt three routinely convert on attempt five or six — for a competitor who kept going. This isn't a motivation problem, it's a structural one: whoever's fielding leads is also underwriting files and coordinating closings, and follow-up is the first thing that slips.

Source: Shape Software lead follow-up data; industry follow-up cadence research (2026).

03

Outside business hours

Over 60% of inquiries land evenings or weekends — when no one's there to catch them.

By the time the office reopens Monday morning, the borrower has often already spoken to another lender. For a shop running two after-hours leads a week, that's over 100 leads a year going stale before anyone even sees them.

Source: Real estate/mortgage lead response benchmarking, 2026 industry data.
